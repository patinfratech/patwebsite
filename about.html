Why we started

The company was set up to build from measured information: survey the ground, model the building, check the work against the drawings.

Construction shapes how people live, work and move. Much of it still runs on methods that waste time, material and money.

Every project will start with a measured survey of the site. The building will be modelled before material is ordered, and the work will be checked against the drawings as it goes up. That is what lets a builder put a date and a cost in writing, and keep to both.

The methods behind this are listed on the Technology page, each marked Planned until it runs on a project.

Four working rules

Rules you can hold us to on every project, from the first site visit to handover, alongside the four documents you agree before work starts.

  • Variations go in writing before the work, not after.

    If the scope or the price has to change, you see the change in writing and agree it before anyone builds it.

  • We build to the drawing and the code.

    When something on site does not match the drawing, we stop, tell you, and put it right before going on.

  • Work stops when it is not safe.

    No date and no saving is worth an injury. A task that cannot be done safely waits until it can.

  • Every estimate is itemised.

    Quantities and rates are listed line by line, so you can check them against the drawings and compare them with other bids.

Start with the site and what you want on it.

Tell us what you want to build and where. Drawings help, but a sketch and a location are enough to begin.
